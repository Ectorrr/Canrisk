Psychological Support

Being diagnosed with a high-impact illness like cancer deeply disrupts the emotional stability of patients and their families. The psychological impact can bring feelings of uncertainty, fear, frustration, and ongoing isolation.


Coping with this process calls for specialized therapeutic support that validates emotions, offers practical resilience tools, and provides a safe, supportive space where the patient doesn't feel alone in their process of adjustment.

Professional Support

Psycho-oncology therapy helps patients manage the anxiety, fear, and depression that arise after diagnosis, promoting a better quality of life and strengthening their mental health throughout treatment.

Family Support

The immediate family also faces an overwhelming emotional burden. Joint sessions guide the family in affectionate communication, empathy, mutual care, and managing everyday stress together.

Support Groups

Sharing experiences with people going through similar situations eases feelings of loneliness, reduces isolation, and encourages a mutual exchange of optimism and useful coping strategies.

Relaxation Techniques

Learning practical exercises in meditation, guided breathing, and emotional regulation helps reduce physical and mental tension, gradually restoring a sense of control over everyday difficulties.

How to support someone with words

A guide for family and friends: small changes in language can make a big difference

Phrases that tend to help

  • "I'm here for you, whatever you need."
  • "You don't have to be strong all the time with me."
  • "How are you feeling today?" (and listening without rushing the answer)
  • "Tell me what you need, I'll take care of organizing it."
  • "I don't know exactly what to say, but I'm with you."

Phrases that are best to avoid

  • "Everything happens for a reason," or comparing their situation to someone else's.
  • "You have to stay positive" as the only response to their distress.
  • "I knew someone who..." followed by a negative outcome.
  • Minimizing what they feel or rushing them to "get over it soon."
  • Avoiding the topic entirely, as if nothing were happening.

A moment to breathe

A simple breathing exercise to reduce anxiety during difficult moments

Inhale... Hold... Exhale
1. Inhale Count to 4 as air slowly enters through your nose.
2. Hold Hold the air in, counting to 4, without forcing it.
3. Exhale Release the air slowly through your mouth, counting to 6.
4. Repeat Repeat the cycle 4 to 6 times, at your own pace.

Common emotional stages

Not everyone goes through these stages, in this order, or with the same intensity. Knowing about them simply helps you understand that whatever you feel is valid.

😶

Denial

Initial difficulty processing the diagnosis.

😠

Anger

Frustration or anger about the situation, sometimes directed at oneself or others.

🤝

Bargaining

Searching for meaning, making internal promises, or thinking "if only...".

😔

Sadness

Moments of deep discouragement while processing the new reality.

🌤️

Acceptance

Gradual adjustment that allows energy to focus on the process and daily wellbeing.

Helplines in El Salvador

Free, confidential psychological support, available to everyone

7071-1302

A free psychological and psychiatric support line, available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, for the entire Salvadoran population.

FOSALUD

2528-9700

Mental health support for young people and adults, with individual or couples therapy. Also available via WhatsApp at 7556-5757.

Salvadoran Red Cross

Psychosocial Support Unit

Free, personalized support for stress, anxiety, deep sadness, or other emotional crises.

If you or someone close to you is having thoughts of self-harm, seek help right away by calling one of these lines or 911. You don't have to go through this alone.
